## Core beliefs — hypotheses to edit, not audience facts

1. **A machine's real capabilities matter more than the headline claim.** One viewer explicitly asks whether UV capability claims are truthful and what the real limits are ([comment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2XTwKMEiq-s&lc=Ugy_BuoiPDVkWNrWxgx4AaABAg)); another calls advertised laser speeds a marketing gimmick outside niche uses ([comment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9mVaKmmhYFc&lc=Ugx8ZZO7K1CD0eVcQOh4AaABAg)). The belief is a hypothesis about these commenters, not a measured channel-wide value.
2. **The right laser depends on the material and job.** Questions about powder-coated tumblers ([comment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2iEHEDWaHOw&lc=UgzMnCqSrJAHN5SdcGx4AaABAg)) and thin marquetry veneer ([comment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eZMyGyXsMys&lc=Ugx7UZelyL0nAC_09wF4AaABAg)) ask for a fit to a specific task. The model-comparison theme has 11 sampled observations; it should not be interpreted as every viewer shopping for the same machine.
3. **Price needs context before a click or purchase.** A viewer specifically values the price-at-recording disclosure ([comment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G9fXsZAbUXs&lc=UgzR-fpSw10CyvZKNFx4AaABAg)). Another asks that waste ink, electricity, and misprints be included in operating-cost math ([comment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNxE6oAopNE&lc=UgxV09faYHbVxKY8pwZ4AaABAg)). This is a candidate editorial principle; it does not establish universal price sensitivity.
